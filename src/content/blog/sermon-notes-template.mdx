## What's on the template, and why

A good sermon notes template (or church notes template, as many churches call it) is short. If it asks too much, you spend the sermon filling boxes instead of listening. Ours keeps only the parts you'll use later:

- **Date, speaker and title.** Months from now, this is how you'll find the sermon again, or look up the recording on your church's website.
- **Scripture.** The passage the sermon is built on. Write the reference first, before the pastor starts, if it's in the bulletin or on the screen.
- **Three main points.** Most sermons have two to four. A few words per point is plenty, plus the verse or story that went with it.
- **Key verse.** The one verse you want to keep. Writing it out in full is the first step to remembering it.
- **Questions** (two-page version). Anything you didn't follow or want to look up. These are often the most useful lines on the page.
- **Application.** One specific thing to do this week. "Be more patient" is hard to act on; "Don't check my phone at dinner on Tuesday" is easy.
- **Prayer** (two-page version). A sentence or two that turns the message into a conversation with God.

## How to use a sermon notes template

1. **Before the service:** print a few and keep them in your Bible or bag. Fill in the date and, if you know it, the Scripture.
2. **During the sermon:** listen first and write second. Jot a few words when the pastor moves to a new point. Leave application and prayer empty for now.
3. **After the service:** take five minutes, in the car or over lunch, to fill in the key verse and the one thing you'll do this week. This short review is what makes the sermon stick, as we explain in <a href="/blog/how-to-remember-sermons">how to remember sermons</a>.
4. **During the week:** reread the page once. Did you do the application? Our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-apply-a-sermon">how to apply a sermon</a> has more ideas if you get stuck.

## Turn it into a sermon notes journal

If you'd like all your notes in one place, make the template into a sermon notes journal. Print a stack of the two-page version, punch holes and keep them in a binder, or take them to a print shop to bind. Add a few blank pages at the front for a running list of key verses. A year of Sundays is about 52 sheets, and flipping back through them is one of the best ways to see what you've learned.

## Using the kids' sermon notes page

The kids' page is made to keep children listening rather than just busy. The word hunt gives them something to do with their ears: a mark every time they hear "God", "Jesus", "love", "pray" or "Bible". The drawing box lets non-readers take part, and the last two lines are best filled in together after church. Ask, "What did you learn about God today?" and write down their answer in their words, not yours.

Kids' ministry leaders and Sunday school teachers are welcome to print a stack for their class.
