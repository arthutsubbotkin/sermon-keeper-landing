## How we picked

We started from the apps people actually find when they search for a Bible notes or Bible journal app, and from the ones Christians already use every day. For each, we read the current App Store listing and official website and looked at five things:

- **Notes on the text.** Can you attach a note to a specific verse, and find it again later?
- **Journaling.** Is there room for longer, personal writing, with or without prompts?
- **Price.** What's free, and what the paid plan costs in the US.
- **Offline.** Whether the app says it works without a connection. Church basements and planes matter.
- **Where your notes live.** Sync, accounts, and anything stated about privacy.

We did not get paid by any app on this list. We left out apps we couldn't verify, and apps that are mainly for recording sermons, which have <a href="/blog/best-sermon-notes-apps">their own guide</a>.

## General-purpose options: Apple Notes and Notion

You don't need a Bible app to keep Bible notes. **Apple Notes** is free on every iPhone, syncs through iCloud, and supports checklists, tags and folders; start each note with the reference and it works as a simple study notebook. **Notion** has a free plan and lets you build your own system, such as a database of passages with tags, though paid plans are $12 per month (US, billed monthly) and it takes time to set up. Neither has any Bible features, so you'll copy verses in yourself.

## Which Bible study app is right for you?

- **You want to start today, for free:** YouVersion.
- **You lead a Bible study or want commentaries:** Logos or Olive Tree.
- **You like writing notes and want verses filled in:** Spirit Notes.
- **You want a guided journal:** Psalmlog.
- **You want your sermon notes and personal study together:** Sermon Keeper.
- **You want full control and no Bible app:** Apple Notes or Notion.
