The full list of the best AI sermon transcription apps: Sermon Keeper (best overall for churchgoers), TurboScribe (best free option for existing recordings), Rev (best for publication-quality accuracy), Notta (best for multilingual churches), Castmagic (best for content repurposing), and VEED (best for video sermons). AI sermon transcription has improved dramatically in the last two years. Tools that once mangled "Deuteronomy" and confused "Romans 8" with a phone number now handle biblical vocabulary with surprising accuracy. But not all transcription tools are equal, and sermons are where the differences show.

We tested six tools on real sermon recordings to compare accuracy, speed, biblical vocabulary handling, and value. Here's what we found.

**What is the best AI note taker for sermons?** Sermon Keeper, if you are in the service yourself. It records the sermon on your iPhone while you tap in short notes, then gives you a transcript and a summary with the outline and every Bible passage the pastor named. If you already have a recording and only need the text, TurboScribe is the best free option.

**What is the best AI voice recorder for sermons?** For recording a sermon and getting it back as text, Sermon Keeper: it records up to three hours, keeps recording with no signal, and transcribes once you're back online. If you only want the audio, Apple's free Voice Memos app does that well.

## Sermon transcription: how it works

Every app on this list follows the same basic steps. Knowing them helps you get a better transcript, whichever tool you pick. If you need a transcript of a sermon that is already on YouTube or in a podcast, or want the free and human options too, see our step-by-step guide to <a href="/blog/how-to-transcribe-sermon">sermon transcription</a>.

<ol class="article-steps">
	<li><strong>Record.</strong> Your phone captures the sermon, or you upload a file you already have (an MP3, a video, a YouTube link in some tools).</li>
	<li><strong>Speech to text.</strong> A speech recognition model turns the audio into words. Good tools are told what kind of vocabulary to expect: in Sermon Keeper, English sermons are sent with a list of Bible and church words, so names like "Deuteronomy" and "Philippians" come out right more often.</li>
	<li><strong>Summary.</strong> A language model reads the transcript and pulls out the structure: the main idea, the points, the verses. This is the step general transcription tools skip and sermon tools are built around.</li>
</ol>

### How accurate is it?

Accuracy depends more on the recording than on the app. A phone near the front, or close to a speaker, gives a clean transcript; a phone in a pocket at the back of a noisy room does not. The usual mistakes are names, place names and verse references said quickly. Sermon Keeper does not invent a summary when it cannot find a sermon in the audio: it tells you, keeps the transcript and lets you try again.

### How long does it take?

The summary arrives after the transcript, once the sermon is over. In Sermon Keeper you tap Finish and wait; the app notes that a long recording can take up to 15 minutes. Tools that work from an uploaded file are similar, and human services like Rev take longer.

### What does it cost?

Prices for each tool are in the reviews above. Free options exist for existing files (TurboScribe, Notta's monthly minutes). Sermon Keeper starts with a free 3-day trial on the yearly plan, then costs $64.99 a year or $6.99 a month.

## How to choose the right tool

Six tools, six different jobs. Match the one you pick to what you actually need to walk away with:

<ul>
	<li><strong>Sermon Keeper</strong> if you want to record and transcribe during church in one step, with offline Bible verses in seven translations and a summary waiting afterward. Free to try. Best for individual churchgoers on iPhone.</li>
	<li><strong>TurboScribe</strong> if you already have the recordings, as an MP3 or a YouTube link, and want accurate transcription for nothing.</li>
	<li><strong>Rev</strong> if the transcript will be published, and you need professional accuracy with theological proofreading.</li>
	<li><strong>Notta</strong> if your church holds services in more than one language.</li>
	<li><strong>Castmagic</strong> if a media team needs one sermon turned into blog posts, social clips, newsletters and show notes automatically.</li>
	<li><strong>VEED</strong> if the job is subtitling sermon videos for YouTube or social media.</li>
</ul>
