## Sermon-Specific Apps

### 1. Sermon Keeper: Best Overall for Churchgoers

Sermon Keeper is the only app here that covers the whole workflow in one place. You record during the service, and it transcribes automatically, produces a summary with key points, and detects Bible verses, all from your iPhone. One tap starts the recording, and you drop timestamped notes while the pastor speaks. Type a reference and the KJV verse autocompletes. Because it was built for sermons rather than meetings, it handles biblical names and theological terms better than the general tools, and Scripture references are linked for you.

Everything is processed on your device and syncs through your own iCloud, so private notes and prayer requests never reach an external server. The free 3-day trial covers the full feature set, with paid plans for unlimited recordings after that. Two real limitations: it is iOS only, with Android in development, and it currently speaks English and Spanish.

### 2. SermonlyAI: Best for Bible Reference Detection

SermonlyAI narrows in on sermon analysis rather than transcription alone. Beyond producing the transcript, it identifies Bible references inside it, analyzes the themes of the message, and tracks what it calls spiritual growth insights across the sermons you save. The library is searchable and everything exports to PDF. Built for religious content from the ground up, it is strong on biblical vocabulary and Scripture detection.

The theme analysis and growth tracking have no equivalent among the general tools, and that is the reason to look at it. What holds it back is the missing timestamped notes while you record, and pricing that is not published: there is a limited free tier, but you will not learn the paid cost until you are inside.

## General-Purpose Tools That Work for Sermons

### 3. TurboScribe: Best Free Option for File Transcription

TurboScribe runs OpenAI's Whisper model and delivers 99.8% accuracy, the highest of any AI-only tool we tested. Its free tier is unusually generous: three files a day at up to 30 minutes each, which works out to 90 minutes of free transcription daily. You can upload MP3, WAV or MP4, or simply paste a YouTube URL, then export to TXT, PDF, SRT or DOCX. Speaker identification is included, and it covers close to 100 languages.

General accuracy is excellent, and Whisper's training data means most biblical names come through correctly. Scripture references are the weak spot: they often arrive as plain digits, so a verse can land looking like "Romans 828" rather than "Romans 8:28". There are no sermon summaries and nothing Bible-specific, it is web-only with no mobile app, and it cannot record live. Paid plans run $10 a month billed annually, or $20 for unlimited.

### 4. Castmagic: Best for Sermon Content Repurposing

Castmagic goes past transcription into content production. It takes one sermon recording and returns blog posts, social captions, newsletters, show notes and pull quotes, with custom templates for the workflows you repeat every week. Transcription covers more than 60 languages and identifies speakers. If your church has to feed several channels from a single Sunday message, this automates the part that normally eats a media volunteer's week.

Speech accuracy is good rather than exceptional, and nothing is tuned for biblical vocabulary, though the drafts come out polished enough to edit rather than rewrite. Cost is the catch: $23 a month on Hobby and $59 on Starter, both billed annually, which is steep for one person. It is aimed at content creators rather than churchgoers, runs in the browser only, and cannot record on a phone.

### 5. Notta: Best for Multilingual Churches

Notta covers 58 languages with real-time translation, which makes it the obvious pick for a multilingual congregation. If your church runs services in Spanish, Korean or Mandarin, it will transcribe them and translate between languages, with AI summaries, speaker identification, and apps on both mobile and web.

English accuracy sits on par with the other general tools, and non-English results are generally reliable, though how reliable depends on the language. The free tier is the problem: 200 minutes a month sounds workable until you notice the five-minute cap per recording, which makes it useless for a sermon without upgrading. Pro costs $8.17 a month billed annually. There is nothing Bible-specific here, and on English audio TurboScribe is the more accurate tool.

### 6. Rev: Best for Publication-Quality Accuracy

Rev is the only option here that offers human transcription alongside AI. The AI tier costs $0.25 a minute, roughly $11 for a 45-minute sermon, and performs about like the other general tools at around 95% accuracy. The human tier costs $1.99 a minute, roughly $90 for that same sermon, and returns 99% accuracy from transcriptionists who verify Scripture references and spell theological terms correctly. Captions and subtitles are included, and there is a mobile app for recording.

That human option is the whole reason to choose Rev, and it earns its price only when the transcript is going somewhere permanent: a church website, a printed study guide, a book. For weekly use the pay-per-minute model adds up fast, and the AI tier holds no sermon-specific advantage over cheaper tools. A free allowance of 45 AI minutes a month lets you test it before committing.

### 7. VEED: Best for Video Sermons

VEED is a video editor with strong transcription attached, rather than the other way round. If your church posts sermon videos to YouTube or social media, it will transcribe the video, generate captions, let you clip highlights, and export in the format each platform expects, all without leaving one tool. It handles more than 125 languages.

Accuracy is good on clear video audio and drops noticeably with poor sound or heavy background music, and nothing is optimized for biblical vocabulary. Pricing starts at $18 a month on Basic and $30 on Pro, with around ten free minutes of subtitles to try it. For transcription alone that is expensive and overkill; the case for VEED is the video workflow, not the text.

## How to Choose the Right Tool

Seven tools, seven different jobs. Match the one you pick to what you actually need to walk away with:

<ul>
	<li><strong>Sermon Keeper</strong> if you want to record and transcribe during church in one step, with Bible verse detection and a summary waiting afterward. Best for individual churchgoers on iPhone.</li>
	<li><strong>TurboScribe</strong> if you already have the recordings, as an MP3 or a YouTube link, and want accurate transcription for nothing.</li>
	<li><strong>Castmagic</strong> if a media team needs one sermon turned into blog posts, social clips, newsletters and show notes automatically.</li>
	<li><strong>SermonlyAI</strong> if you want Bible reference detection and theme analysis rather than a transcript alone.</li>
	<li><strong>Notta</strong> if your church holds services in more than one language.</li>
	<li><strong>Rev</strong> if the transcript will be published, and you need professional accuracy with theological proofreading.</li>
	<li><strong>VEED</strong> if the job is subtitling sermon videos for YouTube or social media.</li>
</ul>
