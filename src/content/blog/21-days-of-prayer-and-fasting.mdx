## What is a 21-day fast?

A fast is a set time of going without something, usually food, so you can give that time and attention to God. Christians have fasted from the beginning: Jesus fasted in the wilderness, and the early church fasted before sending out missionaries. A 21-day fast is simply three weeks of it, and many churches do it together in January to start the year in prayer.

Why 21 days? The number usually comes from the prophet Daniel, who sought God for three weeks:

> "In those days I Daniel was mourning three full weeks. I ate no pleasant bread, neither came flesh nor wine in my mouth, neither did I anoint myself at all, till three whole weeks were fulfilled." (Daniel 10:2–3, KJV)

There's nothing magic about 21. It's long enough to break a routine and short enough to finish. What matters is why you fast, not how long.

## What the Bible says about fasting

Jesus expected his followers to fast, and he talked about how to do it:

> "Moreover when ye fast, be not, as the hypocrites, of a sad countenance: for they disfigure their faces, that they may appear unto men to fast. Verily I say unto you, They have their reward. But thou, when thou fastest, anoint thine head, and wash thy face; That thou appear not unto men to fast, but unto thy Father which is in secret: and thy Father, which seeth in secret, shall reward thee openly." (Matthew 6:16–18, KJV)

Notice he says "when ye fast," not "if." And notice the point: fasting is between you and God, not something to show off.

The prophets add that a fast should change how we treat people, not just what we eat:

> "Is not this the fast that I have chosen? to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke?" (Isaiah 58:6, KJV)

A few more passages to read during your fast:

- **Joel 2:12.** "Turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting, and with weeping, and with mourning." Fasting is about returning to God.
- **Ezra 8:23.** "So we fasted and besought our God for this: and he was intreated of us." Ezra's people fasted before a dangerous journey and asked God for protection.
- **Acts 13:2–3.** The church in Antioch "ministered to the Lord, and fasted," and sent out Barnabas and Saul after they "had fasted and prayed." Fasting often came before big decisions.

## Types of fasts

There's no single right way to fast. Here are the kinds people most often choose for a 21-day church fast:

- **Full fast.** No food, usually for a short time such as one day, with water. Not something to do for 21 days, and not something to start without talking to your doctor.
- **Partial fast.** Giving up one meal a day, or not eating from morning until evening, and using that time to pray. Many people do this for the whole three weeks.
- **Daniel-style fast.** Setting aside certain foods, based on Daniel's example (see below). Churches that do this usually give their own guidelines.
- **Media fast.** No social media, streaming, news or games, or a limit on screen time, and the freed-up time goes to prayer and reading. This works for almost anyone, including people who can't fast from food.

You can combine them, for example skipping lunch and staying off social media in the evenings. Choose something that will actually make you stop and pray, and that fits your health and your family.

## The Daniel fast in the Bible

The "Daniel fast" takes its name from two moments in the book of Daniel. When Daniel was taken to Babylon as a young man, he refused the king's rich food:

> "But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank." (Daniel 1:8, KJV)

He asked instead for a simple diet for ten days: "let them give us pulse to eat, and water to drink" (Daniel 1:12, KJV). Years later, in Daniel 10, he set aside "pleasant bread," meat and wine for three weeks while he prayed (the passage quoted at the top).

That's all the Bible says about it, and it's not a recipe. Today's Daniel-fast food lists and meal plans are guidelines churches have built on those verses. If you're doing one with your church, use the guidelines it gives, and talk to your doctor if you have any health concerns.

## How to plan your 21 days

1. **Decide why you're fasting.** Write it down: a decision you're facing, a relationship, a habit, a person you're praying for, or simply wanting to know God better. You'll come back to it on hard days.
2. **Choose the kind of fast.** Pick one from the list above that fits your health and your life. It's better to choose a smaller fast you'll keep than a big one you'll drop on day three.
3. **Set a daily prayer time.** Use the time you've freed up: the lunch you're skipping, the evening scroll, the morning news. Decide where you'll pray, too.
4. **Follow a daily focus.** Use the 21-day list below so every day has a topic and a short reading. Pair it with a <a href="/blog/bible-reading-plan-2027">Bible reading plan</a> if you want to read more.
5. **Write it down.** A few lines a day is enough: what stood out in the reading, and your prayer. The printable journal below has a page for each day.
