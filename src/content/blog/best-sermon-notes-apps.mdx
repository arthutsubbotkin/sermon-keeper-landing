<p class="article-intro">The best app for taking sermon notes in 2026 is Sermon Keeper. It's the only one built to capture the sermon as it happens, recording the audio while you take timestamped notes, with offline Bible verse autocomplete (KJV) and an AI transcript in a single flow. The other six below are the general tools people reach for, and each does a narrower job well: YouVersion for free verse-linked notes, Olive Tree for a serious study library, Pencil Bible for handwriting on the page, Apple Notes for simple typed notes, Bible Note Journal for devotional journaling, and Voice Memos for just recording the audio.</p>

A few years ago, "sermon notes app" meant whatever note-taking tool you happened to open in church. That still works, but there's now one app built specifically for the moment a sermon is preached, and everything else is a general tool you're adapting. The right pick comes down to <em>what you actually do</em> with a sermon: capture it as it happens, study it afterward, journal your response, or just keep the audio.

We organized this guide as a proper roundup: a comparison table you can scan in ten seconds, one clear "best for" per app, and an honest look at where each one fits. Let's start with what separates a great sermon note app from a generic one.

## What to look for in a sermon note app

Most note apps can hold text. The ones that shine on Sunday solve the specific tension of being in a service, where you want to be present and listening, not heads-down typing. As you compare, these are the features that matter most:

<ul>
	<li><strong>Live capture during the service.</strong> Can you <a href="/blog/how-to-record-sermon-on-iphone">record the sermon as it's preached</a>, so you're not relying on memory or frantic typing? This is the single biggest dividing line between apps.</li>
	<li><strong>Timestamped notes.</strong> When you mark a moment, does the app remember <em>when</em> it happened so you can jump back to that point in the audio later?</li>
	<li><strong>Bible verse autocomplete.</strong> Typing "John 3:16" should be instant, with no leaving the app to look it up &mdash; the best <a href="/blog/bible-verse-detection-apps">Bible verse detection apps</a> pull the full text in as you write.</li>
	<li><strong>AI transcription &amp; summary.</strong> Does it turn the recording into searchable text with the key points pulled out? If that's new to you, here's <a href="/blog/how-to-transcribe-sermon">how sermon transcription works</a>.</li>
	<li><strong>Works offline.</strong> Sanctuaries are notorious dead zones. Recording that works with no signal, plus offline Bible verse lookup (KJV), means you never miss a moment. (And it's worth checking <a href="/blog/is-it-legal-to-record-sermons">whether recording is OK at your church</a> first.)</li>
	<li><strong>Speed and simplicity in the moment.</strong> If setup takes longer than the sermon, you won't use it twice.</li>
</ul>

Only one app on this list does all of the above. The rest each win a single job. Here's how the seven stack up.

## Which sermon note app is right for you?

The "best" app depends on the one job you most want done:

<ul>
	<li><strong>You want to capture the sermon live and revisit it later</strong>, with recording, timestamped notes, verse autocomplete, and a transcript in one flow. That's Sermon Keeper.</li>
	<li><strong>You want free notes tied to the verse</strong> inside the Bible app you already use: YouVersion.</li>
	<li><strong>You take study notes alongside commentaries</strong> during the week: Olive Tree.</li>
	<li><strong>You think best by writing by hand</strong> on the page: Pencil Bible.</li>
	<li><strong>You want the simplest possible typed notes</strong>, already on your phone: Apple Notes.</li>
	<li><strong>Sermon notes are a devotional, journaling habit</strong> for you: Bible Note Journal.</li>
	<li><strong>You just want the raw audio</strong> and nothing else: Voice Memos.</li>
</ul>

For most churchgoers, the deciding factor is staying present in the service without losing the message. That's the gap Sermon Keeper was built to close: record it live, take timestamped notes, autocomplete your verses, and walk away with a transcript and summary you can <a href="/blog/how-to-remember-sermons">actually revisit</a>.
